Trustpilot in detail

What Trustpilot actually is

An open consumer review platform. There is no purchase verification by default, so anyone who claims to be a customer can post. That is a feature for consumers and a problem for brands.

The page itself ranks hard for brand name searches, usually with the star rating showing directly in the result. For a lot of businesses it is the first impression, ahead of their own homepage.

What we do on Trustpilot

  • Invitation flowsAutomated requests timed to the point in your customer journey where satisfaction is highest.
  • Public complaint recoveryWritten replies that show every future reader you fix problems.
  • TrustScore repairVolume from the happy majority, so the average reflects your actual customer base.
  • Policy reportingReviews that break Trustpilot's rules reported properly, with evidence.
  • Verified invitationsUsing Trustpilot's own invitation system so reviews carry the verified badge.
  • Monthly reportingTrustScore, review count and response rate.

How the programme runs

We start by replying. Every live negative review gets a specific, non-defensive public response, because the audience for that reply is the next hundred people who read the page, not the reviewer.

Then invitations, timed to the moment a customer is actually happy rather than at a random point in the month. Timing changes response rate more than anything else.

Where a review genuinely breaks Trustpilot's rules, which usually means it is not from a customer at all, we file the report with evidence.
